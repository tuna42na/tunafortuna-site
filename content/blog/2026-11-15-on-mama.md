## On my mother

I haven't had much time to write because I've been in a state of caregiving and tying up loose ends. I did, however, write my first eulogy, and therefore why shouldn't it be shared? Here is to you, Mama.

## A remembrance of Marianne Fortuna (11-15-2026)

First-time eulogist, long-time listener.

A picture of anyone's life is only as worthy as the frame it's in, and I'd hope that my writing could be a better frame.. but here we are. (Lock the doors)

## My mother was brutally optimistic.

Faced with a diagnosis of ovarian cancer, I only ever heard her ask, "What else can I do?" My mother felt a sense of resilience and duty that carried her through her whole life. I'm still not sure I ever heard her utter the phrase "terminal cancer".

Her resilience was sometimes frustrating. I can remember calling her about troubles in my life, and her answer always seemed to be some version of "stay the course".

Most times I was frustrated by the same simple message, but I also hated how many times it was correct.

My mother always pushed me to "do my best", and it was always an amorphous target. I always knew when I hadn't hit it - she would tell me. She made it known that she expected the best things out of me and believed that I could achieve them.

## My mother was also vulnerable.

Her optimism and persistent mining for a silver lining did not mean that she failed to be open and vulnerable.

I remember her once succumbing to the sentimentality of a particularly poignant ==Walmart== commercial - for emotional context, we were halfway through an episode of Jeopardy.

My mother was shy growing up, from what I understand - I personally wasn't there, so this is all hearsay. I only knew her with the confidence that she had as a mother and in her working life. Several months ago, my Uncle said something offhand that really touched on that truth. He said that the corporate world made her more "outwardly confident". It wasn't until recently that I could see that she still held that shyness. It manifested every time that she would just sit in rooms full of people, content to just listen.

## My mother had a sense of humor.

She sometimes found ways of... we'll say... embellishing the truth, which was entertaining up until she signed you up for something you had never heard of **OR** had to tell a story that you were involved in and hearing for the first time.

My mom's sense of humor never really manifested in jokes per se, but in her way of moving through the world. She inherited a clever way of hearing loss when you would try to catch her on things that she said, almost always accompanied by her asking, "Was I in the room?"

Ironically, I'm not sure my mother ever used the word "ironically" correctly.

## My mother was a businesswoman.

She found her confidence in business, but never seemed to lose her etiquette. A former colleague once said that she "could tell you to go to hell, and you'd be excited about the trip."

She could switch seamlessly from being steadfast and efficient to emotionally receptive and reserved - a trait that I've seen her pass on to my sister.

I like to think that the passion that my mom had for her career has bolstered in me a joy of purpose. I think it is no accident that she was never able to bring herself to retire.

## My mother was a teacher.

My mother, along with my father, showed us how to navigate through life. She continued that guidance with her students after pivoting to teaching later in life.

She fought hard to give us experiences through travel and comfort in education that I'm not sure I ever really got the chance to thank her for. We found our own confidence and community through St. Martin's and beyond.

In the last months of my mom's life, she apologized to Laura and me for a "dicey" upbringing. Unless she was talking about sticking her family in coach for a trip to Europe after her ticket got upgraded, I'm not sure what she was talking about.

### My mother was a Nonna.

I've had the joy of seeing my mother find new life in watching my children grow. Although she only got just short of five years in the role of "Nonna," she always brought a smile to Wylie and Gresham's faces and vice versa. She loved watching them play and would constantly ask for updates even in her lowest times.

One of her goals was to see the boys graduate high school, and I'm sure someday she will.

## My mother was thankful.

My mom was also very adamant that I make sure to be thankful to those around. I'm sure it's unorthodox to do in a eulogy, but in that spirit I want to thank everyone who called, wrote, visited, brought food, you name it. Her last months of life were truly a jubilee, and it was the effort of a village of connections that she had built up. Y'all have done so much to make her feel so loved and valued these last years.
